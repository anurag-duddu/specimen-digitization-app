Field: Date Visited To (date_visited_to). The last date of collecting, only when the label writes a range or an end date, at the precision written.

G44: when the label writes a single collecting date, the step copies it into this field afterwards, as a recorded derivation from Date Visited From. Do not copy it yourself: for a single collecting date answer label_lacks_value. The step never copies a range's start into this field: a written range is yours to resolve.

Your check: parse_date(literal) returns every reading the date's notation allows under the profile's date rules, each as an ISO date at its precision (1946-06-12 for a day, 1946-11 for a month). For this field it settles on a range's end: a reading of a range has the start as iso and the end as end, and the value you may resolve to is the end.
- Resolve only a To date the label writes: the end of a written collecting range ("3-5.IX.1946" and "10-12 Sept. 1946" end on the 5th and the 12th, "VIII-IX.46" ends in September 1946, "3.IX-5.X.1946" on 5 October 1946) or an explicit end date written whole beside a start date ("to 14.X.1946"). For a range the literal is the whole range as written, the same literal as Date Visited From's, and it must be one of organiser_candidates; for an explicit end date it is that date.
- Call parse_date on the exact literal, as written. Resolve with value set to the end it returned; never write a date the check did not return, and never the range's start.
- A date after "det." is the identification date; slide or preparation codes (IV-29-68-4) are never dates. Never join two dates from different events into a range.
- G24: a two-digit year is 19xx only under the profile's century rule, which parse_date applies. G29: a numeric date gives both month-day and day-month readings unless a part over 12 fixes which is which, and parse_date calls it ambiguous (day_month_order_ambiguous): never resolve it to one; resolve on a literal parse_date settles (a named or Roman month), otherwise several_possibilities with both ISO readings.
- A date split over two lines is read as one date only under the rules parse_date applies (via split_lines, year_on_next_line, year_on_previous_line); otherwise its year is missing and it stays open.
- When parse_date cannot read the range as written, answer sources_cannot_resolve and quote the range.
- When readers write the end date differently, parse each; resolve only when they agree or evidence decides.
