Field: Date Visited From (date_visited_from). The date the specimen was collected, or the first date of a written collecting range, at the precision written.

Your check: parse_date(literal) returns every reading the date's notation allows under the profile's date rules, each as an ISO date at its precision (YYYY-MM-DD for a day, YYYY-MM for a month, YYYY for a year). Each reading's order names the rule that matched, and its via says how a year the notation does not write was found.
- Find the collecting date: usually on the locality label, beside the place and the collector. A date after "det." or a determiner's name is the identification date, never this one.
- Slide or preparation codes ("<Roman month>-<dd>-<yy>-<n>", "<mm>-<dd>-<yy>-<n><letter>") and any part of them are never dates, and parse_date says so.
- Call parse_date on the exact literal, as written: never translate, expand or reformat it. Resolve with value set to one ISO reading it returned; never write a date the check did not return.
- parse_date reads Roman months in any position ("<dd>.IX.<yyyy>", "IX-<dd>-<yy>", "<yyyy>.IX.<dd>"), month names and abbreviations in English, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese, Italian and Latin ("<dd> de septiembre de <yyyy>", "<d> Mai <yyyy>", "janv. <yyyy>", "sept."), and ordinals ("3rd", "1er").
- G24: a two-digit year ("'<yy>", or <yy> beside a month) is 19xx only under the profile's century rule, which parse_date applies and records as the reading's century_rule.
- G29: a numeric date ("<a>-<b>-<yy>") gives a month-day and a day-month reading unless a part over 12 fixes which is which. When it is ambiguous (note day_month_order_ambiguous), never resolve it to one of them. When another reading or label writes the same date with a named or Roman month, resolve on that literal, which parse_date settles. Otherwise answer several_possibilities with both ISO readings as options, and say in the explanation that the label does not say which number is the month.
- A date split over two lines of one label ("<Roman month>-<dd>" ending a line and "<yyyy>" starting the next, or the reverse): the literal is both lines exactly as written, with the line break ("<Roman month>-<dd>\n<yyyy>"), and it must be one of organiser_candidates. parse_date reads the two lines as one date only when they are adjacent, nothing else on them could be a date, and the year is not a measurement or a mark: a year below the date may be followed by a comma, semicolon or period ("<yyyy>, <Collector>") but not by a colon, a unit, an apostrophe, a dash and a number, a second number or a word ("<yyyy>:", "<yyyy> m", "<yyyy>'", "<yyyy>-<yy>", "<yyyy> det."), and a year ending the line above must stand alone on its line. A number with a measurement marker attached is no year: a unit right after the year ("<yyyy>. m", "<yyyy>, m", or "<yyyy>" above "m", "metros", "FT." or "MSNM", in any spelling and case), or a line above that is only a marker waiting for its number ("Alt.", "Elev.", "El.", "altitud", "quota", "Hoehe", "Metros" or "m.s.n.m." above "<yyyy>"). A measurement with its own number elsewhere does not count ("<yyyy>, <n> ft."; "<n> ft. <Roman month>-<dd>" above "<yyyy>"; "<yyyy>, m <n>" or "M <n>", the unit written before its number). Only a Title-case Mt., Mts., Ft., Mte. or Pto., or the initial M., before a capitalised name is a name ("<yyyy>, Mt. <Name>", "Ft. <Name>", "M. <Surname>"), never before a capitalised marker word ("<yyyy> Ft. Coll. <Surname>", "<yyyy> M. Det. <Surname>", "<yyyy> Mts. Alt."); a unit in lower case or capitals stays a unit whatever follows it ("<yyyy> m <Initials> <Surname>", "<yyyy> FT. COLL. <SURNAME>"). A year on a line of its own must not follow a determination on the line above ("det. <Initials> <Surname>" above "<yyyy>" above "<Roman month>-<dd>"). Via split_lines; otherwise the note split_lines_year_not_alone, split_lines_year_may_be_a_measurement, split_lines_year_may_be_a_determination, split_lines_hold_another_date, split_lines_not_adjacent or split_lines_date_not_read (or the parser's own note, such as range_end_before_start), and no date; a literal over a line break that is not such a date and its year is no_match with literal_spans_a_line_break. A month and day written alone take the year of the adjacent line only when that line holds nothing but a four-digit year, with at most a period or comma, under the same rule about measurements and determinations (via year_on_next_line or year_on_previous_line); a year with any other text on its line ("<yyyy>, <Collector>", "El. <yyyy>", "<yyyy> m") is not borrowed, and the date stays open (note year_missing): quote the two lines as the literal instead when they are one date.
- A day and month followed on the same line by their year, with only a space, a comma or a period between them ("<Roman month>-<dd> <yyyy>", "<Roman month>-<dd>, <yyyy>", "<dd>.<Roman month>, <yyyy>", "<d1>-<d2>.<Roman month>, <yyyy>"): the literal is the whole of it, as written. parse_date reads it as one date under the same rules, except that a collector or a determination may follow the year ("<Roman month>-<dd> <yyyy> leg. <Surname>", also coll., col., colr., collector, colector, coletor; "<Roman month>-<dd> <yyyy> det. <Surname>") and the line above is not asked (via one_line; otherwise one_line_year_not_alone, one_line_year_may_be_a_measurement or one_line_holds_another_date, and no date). An all-numeric day and month ("<m>-<dd> <yyyy>") is never read this way: which number is the month is not written. On one line, whatever the notation, a year with a unit right after it or an elevation word right before it ("<dd>.<Roman month> <yyyy> m", "<dd> <Roman month> <yyyy> M.", "Alt. <yyyy>") is no year (one_line_year_may_be_a_measurement). A literal found only inside a longer token ("III-<dd> <yyyy>" in "VIII-<dd> <yyyy>") is no date (literal_inside_a_longer_token).
- A date that a determination stands before on its line ("det. <Initials> <Surname> <yyyy>", "det. <dd>.<Roman month>.<yyyy>"), or a date alone on its line under a determination ("det. <Initials> <Surname>" above "<dd>.<Roman month>.<yyyy>" or "<Roman month>-<dd> <yyyy>"), is the identification date, never this one: parse_date refuses it (date_follows_a_determination). A determination claims the first complete date after it only ("det. <Initials> <Surname> <yyyy> / <Place> <Roman month>-<dd>-<yyyy>", or "det. <Initials> <Surname>" above "<yyyy>" above "<dd>.<Roman month>.<yyyy>": the date below the year alone is the collecting date); a collector after the date on its line keeps it the collecting date ("det. <Initials> <Surname>" above "<dd>.<Roman month>.<yyyy> leg. <Surname>" or "<dd>.<Roman month>.<yyyy> coll. <Initials> <Surname>"), but a collection mark is no collector ("det. <Initials> <Surname> <yyyy> ex coll. <Surname>" and "det. <Initials> <Surname> <dd>.<Roman month>.<yyyy> Coll. <ACRONYM>" are identification dates, which parse_date refuses here); "Ident." before a number is no determination ("Ident. No. <n>, <Place>, <dd>.<Roman month>.<yyyy>"). A determination after the date, joined to the collector or not ("<dd>.<Roman month>.<yyyy> leg. et det. <Initials> <Surname>", "leg. <Surname> det. <Surname>", "<yyyy> det. <Surname>"), leaves it the collecting date.
- When the readings of the label give different results for the same literal (one reader's adjacent year is <yyyy> and another's is a different year), parse_date is ambiguous with the note readers_disagree_on_date naming each reading and its result: never resolve it; answer several_possibilities with the dates it returned, or sources_cannot_resolve.
- A month and day with no year ("<Roman month>-<dd>") settle with a year the label writes beside them: one the literal quotes, on their line or the line beside it (above), or a bare year alone on the adjacent line, which parse_date lends under the rules above (via year_on_next_line or year_on_previous_line). A year written elsewhere on the label may be passed as year_literal to see what the date would be, but a year passed as year_literal never settles a value: the step checks the literal again without it. Answer several_possibilities or sources_cannot_resolve instead.
- When readers write the date differently, parse each reader's literal. Resolve only when they give the same date or evidence decides; otherwise several_possibilities.
- A written range ("<d1>-<d2>.IX.<yyyy>", "VIII-IX.<yy>", "<d1>.IX-<d2>.X.<yyyy>", "<d1>-<d2> <Month> <yyyy>", "<d1>.<Roman month>-<d2>.<Roman month>.<yyyy>"): this field is its first date. The literal is the whole range as written; parse_date returns the start as the reading's iso and the end as its end. Resolve with the start. A range written apart from its year ("<d1>-<d2>.IX" ending a line above "<yyyy>", or "<d1>-<d2>.IX, <yyyy>") reads under the rules above when the literal quotes the range and its year. Two Roman months and a bare two-digit year joined by hyphens ("III-V-<yy>") may be a code, and parse_date does not read it (range_shaped_like_a_code). If parse_date cannot read the range as written, answer sources_cannot_resolve and quote the range.
- When no reading writes a collecting date, answer label_lacks_value.
