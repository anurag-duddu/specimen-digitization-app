Field: Date Visited From (date_visited_from). The date the specimen was collected, or the first date of a written collecting range, at the precision written.

Your check: parse_date(literal) returns every reading the date's notation allows under the profile's date rules, each as an ISO date at its precision (YYYY-MM-DD for a day, YYYY-MM for a month, YYYY for a year). Each reading's order names the rule that matched, and its via says how a year the notation does not write was found.
- Find the collecting date: usually on the locality label, beside the place and the collector. A date after "det." or a determiner's name is the identification date, never this one.
- Slide or preparation codes ("<Roman month>-<dd>-<yy>-<n>", "<mm>-<dd>-<yy>-<n><letter>") and any part of them are never dates, and parse_date says so.
- Call parse_date on the exact literal, as written: never translate, expand or reformat it. Resolve with value set to one ISO reading it returned; never write a date the check did not return.
- parse_date reads Roman months in any position ("<dd>.IX.<yyyy>", "IX-<dd>-<yy>", "<yyyy>.IX.<dd>"), month names and abbreviations in English, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese, Italian and Latin ("<dd> de septiembre de <yyyy>", "<d> Mai <yyyy>", "janv. <yyyy>", "sept."), and ordinals ("3rd", "1er").
- G24: a two-digit year ("'<yy>", or <yy> beside a month) is 19xx only under the profile's century rule, which parse_date applies and records as the reading's century_rule.
- G29: a numeric date ("<a>-<b>-<yy>") gives a month-day and a day-month reading unless a part over 12 fixes which is which. When it is ambiguous (note day_month_order_ambiguous), never resolve it to one of them. When another reading or label writes the same date with a named or Roman month, resolve on that literal, which parse_date settles. Otherwise answer several_possibilities with both ISO readings as options, and say in the explanation that the label does not say which number is the month.
- A date split over two lines of one label ("<Roman month>-<dd>" ending a line and "<yyyy>" starting the next, or the reverse): the literal is both lines exactly as written, with the line break ("<Roman month>-<dd>\n<yyyy>"), and it must be one of organiser_candidates. parse_date reads the two lines as one date only when they are adjacent, nothing else on them could be a date, and the year is not a measurement or a mark: the year may be followed by a comma, semicolon or period ("<yyyy>, <Collector>") but not by a unit, an apostrophe, a dash and a number, a second number or a word ("<yyyy> m", "<yyyy>'", "<yyyy>-<yy>", "<yyyy> det."), and a year ending the line above must stand alone on its line (via split_lines; otherwise the note split_lines_year_not_alone, split_lines_hold_another_date or split_lines_not_adjacent, and no date; a literal over a line break that is not such a date and its year is no_match with literal_spans_a_line_break). A month and day written alone take the year of the adjacent line only when that line holds nothing but a four-digit year, with at most a period or comma (via year_on_next_line or year_on_previous_line); a year with any other text on its line ("<yyyy>, <Collector>", "El. <yyyy>", "<yyyy> m") is not borrowed, and the date stays open (note year_missing): quote the two lines as the literal instead when they are one date.
- When the readings of the label give different results for the same literal (one reader's adjacent year is <yyyy> and another's is a different year), parse_date is ambiguous with the note readers_disagree_on_date naming each reading and its result: never resolve it; answer several_possibilities with the dates it returned, or sources_cannot_resolve.
- A month and day with no year ("<Roman month>-<dd>") may take a year the same reading writes elsewhere: pass that year as year_literal.
- When readers write the date differently, parse each reader's literal. Resolve only when they give the same date or evidence decides; otherwise several_possibilities.
- A written range ("<d1>-<d2>.IX.<yyyy>", "VIII-IX.<yy>", "<d1>.IX-<d2>.X.<yyyy>", "<d1>-<d2> <Month> <yyyy>", "<d1>.<Roman month>-<d2>.<Roman month>.<yyyy>"): this field is its first date. The literal is the whole range as written; parse_date returns the start as the reading's iso and the end as its end. Resolve with the start. If parse_date cannot read the range as written, answer sources_cannot_resolve and quote the range.
- When no reading writes a collecting date, answer label_lacks_value.
