Field: Date Visited From (date_visited_from). The date the specimen was collected, or the first date of a written collecting range, at the precision written.

Your check: parse_date(literal) returns every reading the date's notation allows under the profile's date rules, each as an ISO date at its precision (1946-06-12 for a day, 1946-11 for a month, 1946 for a year). Each reading's order names the rule that matched, and its via says how a year the notation does not write was found.
- Find the collecting date: usually on the locality label, beside the place and the collector. A date after "det." or a determiner's name is the identification date, never this one.
- Slide or preparation codes (IV-29-68-4, 10-6-78-1a) and any part of them are never dates, and parse_date says so.
- Call parse_date on the exact literal, as written: never translate, expand or reformat it. Resolve with value set to one ISO reading it returned; never write a date the check did not return.
- parse_date reads Roman months in any position (14.IX.1946, IX-14-46, 1946.IX.14), month names and abbreviations in English, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese, Italian and Latin ("14 de septiembre de 1946", "3 Mai 1946", "janv. 1946", "sept."), and ordinals ("3rd", "1er").
- G24: a two-digit year ('46, or 46 beside a month) is 19xx only under the profile's century rule, which parse_date applies and records as the reading's century_rule.
- G29: a numeric date (4-5-48) gives a month-day and a day-month reading unless a part over 12 fixes which is which. When it is ambiguous (note day_month_order_ambiguous), never resolve it to one of them. When another reading or label writes the same date with a named or Roman month, resolve on that literal, which parse_date settles. Otherwise answer several_possibilities with both ISO readings as options, and say in the explanation that the label does not say which number is the month.
- A date split over two lines of one label ("IV-25" ending a line and "1948" starting the next, or the reverse): the literal is both lines exactly as written, with the line break ("IV-25\n1948"), and it must be one of organiser_candidates. parse_date reads the two lines as one date only when they are adjacent, nothing else on them could be a date, and the year is not a measurement or a mark: a year below the date may be followed by a comma, semicolon or period ("1948, R.D. Mitchell") but not by a colon, a unit, an apostrophe, a dash and a number, a second number or a word ("1948:", "1948 m", "1948'", "1948-49", "1948 det."), and a year ending the line above must stand alone on its line. A number with a measurement marker attached is no year: a unit right after the year ("1948. m", "1948, m", or "1900" above "m"), or a line above that is only a marker waiting for its number ("Alt.", "Elev.", "El.", "altitud", "quota", "Hoehe" or "m.s.n.m." above "1900"). A measurement with its own number elsewhere does not count ("1948, 4800 ft."; "4800 ft. IV-25" above "1948"). A year on a line of its own must not follow a determination ("det. J. Smith" above "1950" above "IV-25"). Via split_lines; otherwise the note split_lines_year_not_alone, split_lines_year_may_be_a_measurement, split_lines_year_may_be_a_determination, split_lines_hold_another_date, split_lines_not_adjacent or split_lines_date_not_read (or the parser's own note, such as range_end_before_start), and no date; a literal over a line break that is not such a date and its year is no_match with literal_spans_a_line_break. A month and day written alone take the year of the adjacent line only when that line holds nothing but a four-digit year, with at most a period or comma, under the same rule about measurements and determinations (via year_on_next_line or year_on_previous_line); a year with any other text on its line ("1948, R.D. Mitchell", "El. 1948", "1948 m") is not borrowed, and the date stays open (note year_missing): quote the two lines as the literal instead when they are one date.
- A day and month followed on the same line by their year, with only a space, a comma or a period between them ("IV-25 1948", "IV-25, 1948", "25.IV, 1948", "3-5.IX, 1946"): the literal is the whole of it, as written. parse_date reads it as one date under the same rules, except that a collector may follow the year ("IV-25 1948 leg. Smith", also coll., col., colr., collector, colector, coletor) and the line above is not asked (via one_line; otherwise one_line_year_not_alone, one_line_year_may_be_a_measurement or one_line_holds_another_date, and no date). A determination right after the year ("1948 det. Smith") is refused. An all-numeric day and month ("4-25 1948") is never read this way: which number is the month is not written. On one line, whatever the notation, a year with a unit right after it or an elevation word right before it ("25.IV 1900 m", "Alt. 1948") is no year (one_line_year_may_be_a_measurement).
- When the readings of the label give different results for the same literal (one reader's adjacent year is 1948 and another's 1949), parse_date is ambiguous with the note readers_disagree_on_date naming each reading and its result: never resolve it; answer several_possibilities with the dates it returned, or sources_cannot_resolve.
- A month and day with no year ("IV-25") settle only with the year the literal itself quotes, on its line or the line beside it (above). A year written elsewhere on the label may be passed as year_literal to see what the date would be, but a year passed as year_literal never settles a value: the step checks the literal again without it. Answer several_possibilities or sources_cannot_resolve instead.
- When readers write the date differently, parse each reader's literal. Resolve only when they give the same date or evidence decides; otherwise several_possibilities.
- A written range ("3-5.IX.1946", "VIII-IX.46", "3.IX-5.X.1946", "10-12 Sept. 1946", "12.VI-3.VII.1946"): this field is its first date. The literal is the whole range as written; parse_date returns the start as the reading's iso and the end as its end. Resolve with the start. A range written apart from its year ("3-5.IX" ending a line above "1946", or "3-5.IX, 1946") reads under the rules above when the literal quotes the range and its year. Two Roman months and a bare two-digit year joined by hyphens ("III-V-46") may be a code, and parse_date does not read it (range_shaped_like_a_code). If parse_date cannot read the range as written, answer sources_cannot_resolve and quote the range.
- When no reading writes a collecting date, answer label_lacks_value.
