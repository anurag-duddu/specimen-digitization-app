# The field experts' instructions on the units path (instructions(key, units=True)). Under
# "== <file>", each "<<< " line is a text of that file, found there exactly once, and the
# ">>> " line after it is the text that stands in its place on the units path; a ">>>" line
# alone leaves that line out. The common.txt label notations left out are those the specimen
# ontology's text, which the units path's instructions carry, gives too.
== common.txt
<<< - first_pass, when present: for each label whose readers differ (by label number), the opinion of a model that compared its readings against the label image: the reader it preferred (preferred: a reading's name, or null when it preferred none; that reading's input_source is decided_transcript, every other's raw_reading), its rationale, a note on each reader (reader_notes), and each material difference with the reader whose text it judged the image supports there (verdict: a reading's name, neither or uncertain). It is advice, not evidence: every reading stays a raw transcription, and another reader's text is a possibility the first pass may have missed, which you may look up and settle on.
>>> - units, when present: each label's text cut into reading units (by label number, each unit with its first and last line), by a model that judged the label image, or by aligning the readings where that model gave none or where a person's text replaced them. Each unit gives the text there of each reading it was cut from (readers, by reading name; "" where a reading has nothing there), or, where it was cut from two or more readings and each writes the same text there, that text once (text): the label's machine readings, or, where a person decided the label, the person's text alone, under the name of the reading that holds it. Each unit gives a judgement of the label image there that may be uncertain (image: one reader's text, uncertain or not visible), and, where it has them, its meaning (means), its part of a field's value (role), the fields it could belong to (fields: advice only) and a note. The literal is still copied from the readings, and a field's text may be a whole unit, a part of one or run over several.
<<< 4. Never pick between readings that disagree on fluency or on your own reading of them: evidence decides, and evidence is a source's match, readers that agree, or another label of this specimen that writes the text. Readers of a label whose texts differ only in letter case or spacing ("shrubs" and "Shrubs", "sp. 30" and "Sp.30") agree: the literal is the text of the reader the first pass preferred, else of the first reader (2A before 2B, 2B before 2C), copied exactly. When the readers of a label write different text, three things can back one reader's text: (a) you asked your sources about each reader's text, a success answer confirms exactly that one by its own name (for a place, or by its expansion), and every other found no match; (b) another label of this specimen, read by two or more readers who agree, writes it; (c) for a place, no reader's text is found by its own name, a candidate whose own name you asked is one letter from that one, and every other found no match. (a) settles on that reader's text whichever reader the first pass preferred, also where its preferred reader writes nothing for this field; but for a place where the preferred reader writes a text one letter from the confirmed one, at a difference whose verdict names it, the label writes the preferred reader's spelling: give that as the literal, naming its reading, and the source's candidate as the value. (b) and (c) settle on it too, except where the first pass's verdict at that difference (first_pass) names the reader it preferred: the first pass judged the image there, so answer several_possibilities with both texts, for a person to choose. For a place, readers whose texts your lookups confirm as the same place (one candidate at the field's level with one authority_id, as "<Town>," and "<Town>" are) settle too: give one reader's text as the literal with that authority_id. When nothing backs another reader's text, follow the first pass: the preferred reader's text settles its label, and a label whose preferred reader writes nothing for this field does not write it. When the first pass preferred no reader and nothing backs one reader's text, answer several_possibilities with each reader's text. A field on several labels settles only when every label settles to the same value.
>>> 4. Never pick between readings by fluency or by your own reading of them. Whenever a reader writes a text for this field, answer with the literal exactly as a reader wrote it, the readings that hold it, and the source answers you cite. Readers whose texts differ only in letter case or spacing ("shrubs" and "Shrubs", "sp. 30" and "Sp.30") agree: answer the text as one of them writes it. Two or more readers who write one text give an answer, not a choice: answer that text. Answer several_possibilities, with each reader's text as its options, only where you find no text to answer. Looking a place or name up in your sources is how a source can settle a text the image does not. A label whose transcript a person decided (person_decided) has that text. The code decides from the image judgements, the source answers your lookups returned and the readers' agreement whether a value is cleared, marked for a person to check, or left open, and it refuses an answer it cannot settle, saying why.
<<< On a label whose first pass preferred no reader, every reader of that label must write it, with the same text around it in the part of the line between commas or semicolons (or the whole line), letter case and spacing aside: "trap" beside another reader's "light trap" does not settle. Otherwise answer several_possibilities with each reader's text.
>>> The code decides, as rule 4 says, whether such a literal settles.
<<< - "Prov.", "Dept.": province, department; a word of the unit's name, not the name itself.
>>>
<<< - "Mt.": mount, part of the place's name. "Is.": island or islands. "nr.": near. "E. slope", "W. side": a direction qualifying the place that follows.
>>>
<<< - "leg.", "coll.", "Coll.": collected by, marking the collectors. "det.": determined by, marking who identified the specimen.
>>>
<<< - A date can be a range ("<d1>-<d2>.IX.<yyyy>", "VIII-IX.<yy>", "<d1>.IX-<d2>.X.<yyyy>"): its first date is the start and its second the end.
>>>
<<< - A two-digit year ("'<yy>", "-<yy>") is read as 19<yy> under the profile's century rule.
>>>
<<< - "m", "ft.", "'", "alt.", "el.": the unit or heading of an elevation. "ca.", "c.": about; the value is approximate.
>>>
<<< - "?": the value before it is uncertain as written.
>>>
<<< - Slide or preparation codes, a month, a day, a two-digit year and a slide number joined by hyphens ("<Roman month>-<dd>-<yy>-<n>", "<mm>-<dd>-<yy>-<n><letter>"), are never dates, nor is any part of them.
>>>
== collectors.txt
<<< - Collectors usually follow "leg.", "coll.", "Coll.", "Collected by" or "Collector(s):". The literal is the names as written, in the written order, with their initials, spelling and joiners ("<Initials> <Surname>", "<Surname> & <Surname>"), without the marker itself.
>>> - Collectors usually follow "leg.", "coll.", "Coll.", "Collected by" or "Collector(s):". The literal is the names as written, in the written order, with their initials, spelling and joiners ("<Initials> <Surname>", "<Surname> & <Surname>"), without the marker itself, also where one unit holds the names and the marker ("<Initials> <Surname> leg."): the literal is then the names inside it.
<<< - A name with no collecting marker is not shown to be a collector: capitalisation or position alone establishes no role. Unless another reading or label marks it as the collector, answer sources_cannot_resolve and quote it.
>>> - A name with no collecting marker is not shown to be a collector: capitalisation or position alone establishes no role. Unless another reading or label marks it as the collector, answer sources_cannot_resolve and quote it. A unit's note is never a reason to refuse a name every reader writes.
<<< - Resolve when the readings agree on the names. When the readers of a label write a name differently and another label of this specimen, read by two or more readers who agree, writes one reader's names, resolve with those names, unless the first pass's verdict at that difference (first_pass) names the reader it preferred: then answer several_possibilities with both. Otherwise resolve with the names of the reader the first pass preferred (input_source decided_transcript). When the first pass preferred no reader and no other label writes one reader's names, answer several_possibilities with each reader's text.
>>> - Resolve with the names the readings write (rule 4), also where the readers write them differently: answer the reader's names the units support, naming the readings that hold them. Answer several_possibilities, with each reader's text, only where you find no names to answer.
