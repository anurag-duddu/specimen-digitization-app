Field: Precise Location (precise_location). The locality as the label writes it: the most specific place text, such as "E. slope Mt. <Mountain>, nr. <Town>".

This field is verbatim locality text (PRD 515). Its value is always the literal itself: never replace it with a source's name, never expand an abbreviation, never translate. Leave value and authority_id empty.

Your sources: lookup with geolocate, tgn (Getty Thesaurus of Geographic Names), wikidata or nga (the US gazetteer of foreign names). A query is place words only, comma separated, from the place out to its country ("<Place>, <Province>, <Country>"). GEOLocate needs the place and its country, with at most a state and a county between them; the gazetteers search the first name and return its parents. Never send collectors, dates, elevations, numbers or whole label text.
- If a source cannot be reached (an error status and no evidence_id), decide with the sources that answered when they settle the field under these rules, and say in the explanation which source did not answer.
- Use the sources only to check that the text names a real place consistent with the label's country and province, and to decide between readers who read a place word differently. A source result never changes this field.
- The literal is everything the label writes as the locality: directions ("E. slope"), "nr.", "Mt.", distances and named places, copied exactly from one reading. Leave out what belongs to other fields: country, province, county or city written as such, dates, collectors and elevations. Text you read from the readings that holds an elevation, a date or a range of them ("<Town>, <n>ft.", "<Place>, <n> m, N slope", "<Place>, alt. <n>") is sent back; a distance ("<n> m N of", "<n> m de", "<n> m along <Place>", "<n> m upstream of <Place>", "<n> m above the <Place>") is locality text, and so is a number right after a road, km, hill, farm or estate word ("km <n>", "Hill <n>").
- A locality the label writes over consecutive lines ("E. slope Mt." over "<Mountain>, nr. <Town>") may run over them, unlike any other field: copy those lines exactly as a reading writes them, with each line break (or a space for each), whole words at both ends. It may join the organiser's candidates for those lines in their order on the label, but it never cuts one, and every reading you name must write the same lines.
- Resolve when the readings agree on the locality text, or when a source decides between readers' variants of a word; the literal is copied from a reading that has the decided text.
- When readers write the locality differently and nothing decides, answer several_possibilities with each reader's text.
- When no reading writes a locality more precise than the other place fields, answer label_lacks_value.
