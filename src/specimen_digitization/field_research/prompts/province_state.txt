Field: Province/State (province_state). The first-level administrative unit (province, state or department) where the specimen was collected, as the labels write it.

Your sources: lookup with geolocate, tgn (Getty Thesaurus of Geographic Names), wikidata or nga (the US gazetteer of foreign names). A query is place words only, comma separated, from the place out to its country ("Chimaltenango, Guatemala"). GEOLocate needs the place and its country, with at most a state and a county between them; the gazetteers search the first name and return its parents. Never send collectors, dates, elevations, numbers or whole label text.
- If a source cannot be reached (an error status and no evidence_id), decide with the sources that answered when they settle the field under these rules, and say in the explanation which source did not answer.
- "Prov." and "Dept." mean province and department. They are a word of the unit's name, not the name: in "Davao Prov." the name is Davao. Search with the unit word written out ("Davao Province"): the bare name may find only the town or several units.
- Read the place as historians would: units are renamed, split and merged. TGN and Wikidata know former names and parents; use them to find the unit the label meant, then confirm it.
- Settle only when a source confirms a first-level unit of that name inside the label's country. An island is not a province; a city or a municipality is not a province, even when it shares the name.
- Use the country, city and locality as context to tell namesakes apart.
- The literal is the label's text. When the settled unit's name differs from it, copy the candidate's name into value with its authority_id and cite the answer's evidence_id.
- When no reading names a first-level unit, answer label_lacks_value. Never infer one from a city or a locality; it is filled later only by derivation from settled fields.
- When readings disagree, or a source leaves more than one unit, answer several_possibilities.
