Field: Habitat (habitat). The habitat, host or substrate the label writes, such as "under <substrate>", "on <host>" or "<vegetation> forest", copied as written.

No source checks it: this field settles from the readings alone.
- Take only text with ecological context: where or on what the specimen lived or was found. A locality, a person, a date, an elevation, a determination or a preparation code is not a habitat; a slide code such as "<Roman month>-<dd>-<yy>-<n>" never is (G45).
- A collecting method or device (a trap, a net, a light, beating, sweeping, Berlese, malaise, pitfall, bait, by hand, cut branch, and the like) is the collection method, never the habitat, wherever it stands on the label and however the organiser offered it. When the only text a candidate or a reading offers for this field is a method, the label lacks a habitat: answer label_lacks_value.
- A host is this field's value: the animal or plant the specimen was taken from is what it lived on. The label notations above mark a host "[no field]" and "never its taxon"; for this field that means one thing only, that a host is never the specimen's taxon. A host the label writes is the habitat: answer it, and never answer label_lacks_value for a label that writes one.
- A host is the name written after a host marker ("Host <name>", "Ex. <name>", "ex <name>", "On <name>"), on the marker's line or on the lines right below it. It is also the name of an animal that is no insect (a mammal or a bird, by its scientific name, "<Genus>" or "<Genus> <species>", or by its common name or a group word for such animals) written with no marker: the specimens are insects, so the specimen's own taxon is an insect's name, never another animal's. Such a name is the host also where the units' fields advice or note, the organiser or other_fields give it as the taxon or say that no field holds it. When you cannot tell whether a name written with no marker names an insect or another animal, answer sources_cannot_resolve and quote it.
- Copy the host whole, as the label writes it: every word of the name (the genus, the species, a subspecies, and the words the label writes with them, such as "sp." with its letter or number), with a written "Ex.", "Ex:", "ex" or "On" before the name kept in the literal. A heading "Host" or "Host:" before the name, in any letter case, is left out: the literal starts at the name, and an answer that starts with that heading is refused. A sex sign after the name is not part of it.
- A host or a habitat the label writes over consecutive lines ("<Genus>" over "<species>"; a word broken by a hyphen at a line's end) may run over them, whatever the shared rules say of two lines: copy those lines exactly as a reading writes them, with each line break (or a space for each) and the hyphen where the label breaks a word, whole words at both ends, and every reading you name must write the same lines. Never answer the first line alone of a name the label goes on with on the next.
- Copy it exactly as written, in the label's language: never translate, expand or correct it (a habitat written in Spanish stays in Spanish).
- Resolve with the text the readings write (rule 4), also where the readers write it differently: answer the reader's text the units support, naming the readings that hold it. Answer several_possibilities, with each reader's text, only where you find no text to answer.
- When no reading writes a habitat, answer label_lacks_value.
