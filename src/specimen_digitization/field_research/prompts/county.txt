Field: County (county). The country's second-level administrative unit where the specimen was collected, as the labels write it: a county, or whatever unit the country's local government holds at that level instead (a municipio, where that is the second level).

Your sources: lookup with tgn (Getty Thesaurus of Geographic Names), wikidata or nga (the US gazetteer of foreign names). A query is place words only, comma separated, from the county out to its country ("<County>, <State>, <Country>"). The gazetteers search the first name of a query and return its parents. Never send collectors, dates, elevations, numbers or whole label text.
- If a source cannot be reached (an error status and no evidence_id), decide with the sources that answered when they settle the field under these rules, and say in the explanation which source did not answer.
- The level comes from the sources, never from the unit's name or the country: a second-level unit is one Getty TGN types "second level subdivisions (political entities)" or "counties", NGA designates A.ADM2, or Wikidata gives the kind "county" or "second-level administrative division". A first-level unit (a province, a state, a department) is never the county.
- A town and the second-level unit around it often share a name, so a label's "<Town>" may name both. When a source confirms a second-level unit of the label's own text inside the label's province or state and country, settle the county on it, even though the city settles on the town of that name.
- Settle only when a source confirms a second-level unit of that name inside the label's province or state and country. When the label writes a province or state, the county settles only inside it once it is settled.
- A name the label writes as its province ("<Province>", "<Province> Prov.") is the province, never the county. When a source also finds the label's text as a first-level unit and the label writes no county or municipality unit word with it ("Co.", "Mun."), the sources cannot tell which it means: answer several_possibilities.
- Read the place as historians would: units are renamed and redrawn. Use TGN and Wikidata for former names and parents, then confirm.
- Use the country, province, city and locality as context to tell namesakes apart.
- The organiser's candidate may be another field's text or only a unit word ("Co."): the literal is then the text the reading writes for the county, or answer label_lacks_value when it writes none.
- The literal is the label's text ("<County> Co.", "<Town>"); when it writes a unit word, search with the unit word written out ("<County> County"). When the settled unit's name differs from it, copy the candidate's name into value with its authority_id and cite the answer's evidence_id.
- When no source holds a second-level unit of a name the labels write, answer label_lacks_value. Never take the unit around a place of another name, nor one found from a point; it is filled later only by derivation from settled fields.
- When readings disagree, or a source leaves more than one second-level unit, answer several_possibilities.
