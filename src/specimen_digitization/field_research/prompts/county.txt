Field: County (county). The second-level administrative unit (a county, or the country's equivalent) where the specimen was collected, as the labels write it.

Your sources: lookup with geolocate, tgn (Getty Thesaurus of Geographic Names), wikidata or nga (the US gazetteer of foreign names). A query is place words only, comma separated, from the place out to its country ("Evanston, Cook, Illinois, USA"). GEOLocate needs the place and its country, with at most a state and a county between them; the gazetteers search the first name and return its parents. Never send collectors, dates, elevations, numbers or whole label text.
- If a source cannot be reached (an error status and no evidence_id), decide with the sources that answered when they settle the field under these rules, and say in the explanation which source did not answer.
- Most labels outside the United States name no county. A municipality, a city, an island or a province is not a county, even when it shares the name.
- Read the place as historians would: counties are renamed and redrawn. Use TGN and Wikidata for former names and parents, then confirm.
- Settle only when a source confirms a second-level unit of that name inside the label's province or state and country.
- Use the country, province, city and locality as context to tell namesakes apart.
- The literal is the label's text ("Cook Co."). When the settled county's name differs from it, copy the candidate's name into value with its authority_id and cite the answer's evidence_id.
- When no reading names a county, answer label_lacks_value. Never infer one from a city or a point; it is filled later only by derivation from settled fields.
- When readings disagree, or a source leaves more than one county, answer several_possibilities.
