You fill the record of one insect specimen slide for the Field Museum Insects collection.

INPUT. One slide. First the image of every label on the slide (Label 1, Label 2, ...), then the transcriptions of each label by two machine readers (reader A, reader B). The readers copy text; either may be wrong, and both may be wrong the same way. The label image decides. Use only the images and the readings: no lookups, no outside knowledge of the specimen, the collection or the collector.

OUTPUT. One call of the tool record_fields: all 20 fields below, in this order, each as {value, evidence}.
- value: the field's value as the finished record should show it, in the form given below, or null.
- evidence: the label text the value rests on, copied from the label with its label number ("L2: <the words as written>"), at most about 15 words. Empty when value is null.
- null with evidence "UNSURE: <short reason>" when a label carries text for the field that you cannot settle (illegible, readers differ and the image does not decide, a day and month that could be either way). Null with empty evidence when the labels carry nothing for the field. Never guess to complete a record; a field the labels do not carry stays null. Most slides leave several fields null.

RULES FOR EVERY FIELD
- One slide is one specimen with one collecting event and one identification. A field's text may be on any label; its parts may be on different labels (the genus on one label, the species on another): join them.
- Free text is copied as written: letters, digits, punctuation, abbreviations, language. Never correct, expand, translate or complete it. Words that only name a field (a printed "Locality", "Host", "Collector", "Loc:") are not part of its value.
- A slide or preparation code (a date-like string with a trailing number or letter, such as "<Roman month>-<dd>-<yy>-<n>" or "<mm>-<dd>-<yy>-<n><letter>") is no date, no collection code, no habitat, no method, no collector and no Verbatim D/T/S, in whole or in part.
- Dates are ISO: YYYY-MM-DD, or YYYY-MM when only the month is written, or YYYY when only the year is. A Roman numeral is a month. A two-digit year belongs to the 1900s. A numeric date whose two orders give different dates (both numbers 12 or under, nothing else fixing the order) is UNSURE, never one reading.
- Numbers (elevations, the catalogue number) carry no unit and no prefix.

THE 20 FIELDS
1 fmnh_ins_number. The catalogue number: the digits printed after "FMNH INS" or "FMNHINS", usually on a barcode label. Digits only, as printed (seven digits on these labels), leading zeros kept, never a changed digit. A slide code, collection code, date or record number is not one.
2 collection_code. A code the label marks as the collection's or the collecting event's. Owner's ruling: the only collection code is the printed heading "LEWIS COLLECTION". Everything else is null: slide and preparation codes, a collector's or field number, a determination label's number, lot numbers, an institution's name or stamp, "CNHM".
3 country. The country's current English name. A former name or abbreviation the label writes is given as the modern name. When the labels name no country but name a state or province that lies in one country only, give that country (evidence: the state's text). Never infer a country from a collector, a taxon or a town.
4 province_state. The first-level unit (province, state, department) the label writes, without its unit word ("Prov.", "Dept.", "State"). A printed heading that names a state for the specimens it holds ("<Group> of <State>") gives it when no other label writes one, unless the heading is struck out. An island, a mountain, a region, a town or a county is not a province.
5 county. The second-level unit (county, or the municipio where that is the second level) the label writes, without "Co." or "County". A province or state is never the county. Null when no label names one.
6 city. The city, town or municipality the labels name as the collecting place. A town written only as the origin of a distance ("<n> mi. N of <Town>", "<n> km W <Town>") is not the city: it stays in Precise Location and City is null. A mountain, island, river, estate or province is not a city; a name written with "Prov." or "Dept." is the province.
7 precise_location. The label's whole locality as written: every line of its locality text joined with single spaces, with its directions ("E. slope"), "nr.", distances, named places, the higher places those lines write (town, province or state, country, notations included), an elevation written on those lines and "CNHM." where a locality line writes it. Never a collector, a date, a code, a taxon or any other field's text; lines holding only those are left out. Never only the first line. Not replaced by a gazetteer's name.
8-11 elevation_from_m, elevation_to_m, elevation_from_ft, elevation_to_ft. Numbers. The "from" fields hold the lower, or only, elevation; the "to" fields the upper end of a written range, or the same number as "from" when one elevation is written. The "m" fields hold metres: the number the label writes in metres, else the written feet times 0.3048, rounded to the hundredth. The "ft" fields hold feet: the number written in feet, else the written metres divided by 0.3048, rounded to the hundredth. Units are in any spelling ("m", "msnm", "m a.s.l." are metres; "ft.", "'", "pies", "Fuss" are feet). "ca." and "alt." are not part of the number. A number with no unit written anywhere is not an elevation here: never guess the unit from its size or the place; UNSURE. A depth, a distance or a count is not an elevation. "Mt" may be Mount.
12 habitat. Where or on what the specimen lived or was found, as written: "under <substrate>", "on <host>", "<vegetation> forest". A bare animal name written on its own is the host and goes here as written. The whole phrase, all its words. A trap, net, light or other technique is the collection method, not the habitat. A locality, person, date, elevation, determination or code is not a habitat.
13 collection_method. How it was collected, as written (a trap, a technique, an apparatus). Where it lived is the habitat.
14 date_visited_from. The collecting date, or the first date of a written range: usually on the locality label beside the place and the collector. A date after "det." or a determiner's name is not it. A year or a month and year alone are given at that precision.
15 date_visited_to. The last date of a written range or a written end date. When one collecting date is written, the same date as date_visited_from. When only a year or month is written, the same.
16 collectors. The people who collected it, as written: their initials, spelling and order, joined as the label joins them, without the marker word ("leg.", "coll.", "col."). A name is a collector when a marker ("leg.", "coll.", "col.", "collected by") says so; a name with no marker is not shown to be one (UNSURE). A determiner ("det."), a preparer and an institution are not collectors. Initials followed by a number are a collector's number: no collector.
17 verbatim_dts. Only the collecting date or time when it is NOT an exact date, copied as written: a year alone, a month and year, a range of months, a season, "am", "pm", "sunrise", "daylight". Where the date is written to the day, null. Such a partial date is also the collecting date of fields 14 and 15 at the precision written (a range of months: its first month in 14, its last in 15).
18 taxon. The scientific name the labels give the specimen, at the rank written, copied as written with its author and year when written (genus capitalised, epithets lower case): a genus alone, genus and species, genus species subspecies. "sp." or "cf." after a genus gives the genus. A common name is not a taxon. A name written after a host marker ("Host", "ex", "from") names the host and goes to habitat. A non-insect animal's name (a mammal, a bird) written alone is a host, not the taxon. When the labels write no scientific name of the specimen, null.
19 identified_by_irn. Always null. In evidence copy the determiner's text exactly as written after "det." when a label names one; otherwise empty.
20 date_identified. The date written with the determination: after "det." and the determiner's name, or in the date slot of a determination label. A collecting date is never it, even when it is the only date.

Answer with the tool call only.
